# Northing Studio — Product Plan

> [!NOTE] **Source:** `Research Reports/2026-09-12 Gumroad Digital Product Research Report.md` (Gumroad only; Etsy research paused by the owner), plus the evidence behind it in `research/`. **Scope:** a buildable launch plan — brand kit, ladder, selection, full briefs, build specs, listings, calendar, risks and handoff. Every major decision cites its evidence; everything else is labelled **[Assumption]**.

# Executive summary

**What we'll launch:** seven products and one bundle, in one warm editorial brand system, each shipped as a printable PDF (A4 + US Letter) and a hyperlinked tablet PDF, with Notion tiers where they genuinely add something.

| Order | ID | Name | Format | Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | PB-006 | The One-Page Year | print + tablet | **free** |
| 2 | PB-022 | The 10-Minute Life Audit | print + tablet | **free** |
| 3 | PB-001 | The Focus Audit | print + tablet (+ Notion) | **$9** |
| 4 | PB-003 | The Balance Audit | print + tablet + Notion | **$12 / $19** |
| 5 | PB-002 | The Compass System | print + tablet + Notion | **$19 / $29** |
| 6 | PB-004 | The 90-Day Kaizen Program | workbook + wall tracker | **$24** |
| 7 | BN-02 | The Life Direction Set | bundle of the four paid | **$49** |
| 8 | PB-005 | The Review Ritual *(wave 2)* | print + tablet (+ Notion) | **$15** |

**Why this, and why now.** The research found Gumroad's planning market is a Notion market run on free tiers, where the headline numbers are mostly free claims. Two lanes are open at the top: **guided method products** (audits, workbooks, review rituals) and a **warm, light, editorial look delivered in multiple formats**. Our categories sit in both. The single occupant of that quadrant, Ideas To Thrive, is new and has almost no proof — 9 ratings on its $49 flagship.

**The three decisions that shape everything else:**

1. **Position:** warm, light, editorial + guided method + multi-format. Nobody at the top of Gumroad is there, and only one warm editorial thumbnail exists in the entire competitor set.
2. **Method over trackers.** Trackers are saturated; teaching is rare. Every product ships a named mechanism — the Floor, the Restart Page, the Vital Few, Standing Rules — and a visible payoff page.
3. **Show everything.** Competitors sell $47–$149 products with one to three images and no interior previews. A complete 10-slot image set per listing is our cheapest durable advantage.

**What it costs in effort:** **237 hours** of solo build work across brand system, seven products and seven listing image sets (+22 h if all three Notion tiers ship at launch). At 20 hours a week that is roughly **12 weeks to the full lineup**, with the first paid product live in week 4.

**The first milestone:** by the end of **week 4**, the two free products and PB-001 are live, the brand grid reads as one shop, and we have our first ratings — which is what drives Gumroad Discover ranking.

> [!WARNING] **Two naming decisions were forced by the evidence and cannot be reversed without legal risk.** "Wheel of Life" is a live US trademark covering printed personal-development and goal-setting materials, so PB-003 is renamed and the phrase is banned from our titles, tags and pages. "Life Compass" is crowded on both platforms and is also the name of a therapy worksheet, so PB-002 is renamed. Details in Risks & compliance.

# Evidence digest

The fifteen findings this plan is built on. Sources are the report's sections plus the seller files and audits behind them.

> [!INSIGHT] The market's weak spot is consistent across all fifteen sellers: **they sell tools, not methods, and they hide the inside of the product.** Every decision below exploits one of those two gaps.

## Demand and what actually sells

1. **Free tiers inflate every headline number.** Most flagship listings include a $0 tier and Gumroad's `sales_count` counts free checkouts. Heyismail's "$3.9M" is an upper bound; the confirmed paid floor is ≈$50k. *(Report §Executive summary #1; `research/01-seller-discovery.md` §Revenue caveat.)* **Consequence:** we treat claim counts as audience signals, never as revenue evidence.
2. **Guided PDF products genuinely sell on Gumroad when framed as a program or workbook.** *Live Intentionally* 13,089 × $19.99 ≈ $266k; *The M.E.A.N. Workbook* 4,475 × $47 ≈ $230k; *7-Day Reset* 2,272 × $7. *(Report §Best-seller teardown.)* **Consequence:** PDF is a viable primary format — but as a *program*, not as a "planner".
3. **PDF planners are unproven on Gumroad.** JNKxStudio's 920-page GoodNotes planner was abandoned with 0 ratings. *(Report §Seller profiles 03.)* **Consequence:** tablet PDFs ship as a *format tier*, never as the flagship promise. Tablet-planner demand gets tested on Etsy later.
4. **Nobody sells a named 80/20 or focus audit.** The nearest things are one day inside *7-Day Reset* and an $89 time-audit book. *(Report §Category analysis; `research/categories/gumroad-categories.md`.)* **Consequence:** PB-001 is the lowest-competition launch and goes first among paid products.
5. **Purpose and self-audit are thin, not empty.** The best ikigai product has 785 sales; the whole purpose-compass category shows 8,881 known sales. *(Category analysis.)* **Consequence:** real but modest demand — price at $9–19, don't bet the flagship on volume.
6. **The goal-workbook category has zero strong products.** 15 products found, none with ≥1,000 sales or ≥100 ratings. *(Category analysis.)* **Consequence:** low competition *and* unproven demand — which is why PB-004 borrows the proven *program* framing instead of the "workbook" framing.
7. **Trackers are everywhere; teaching is rare.** Habit, finance and goal trackers run to 20k–50k claims each, almost none with a method, review ritual or decision step. *(Observed across 13 sellers.)* **Consequence:** this is the gap the whole brand occupies.

## What buyers complain about — these are product requirements

8. **Setup friction.** "It takes some reading to adapt…", "It took 10–15 min to adapt to it." *(Organized dashboard buyers, report §Buyer voice.)* → every product opens with a "Start here" page and a stated time cost.
9. **Complex systems rate worse.** Chris's Habit Hub 10% 1★; Atul's finance 15% 1★ and habit 22% 1★; JNKxStudio's starter pack 33% 1★. *(Report §Buyer voice.)* → fewer, deeper pages; a "minimum viable use" path; scope stated honestly.
10. **The early days are hard.** "the first 21 days were very hard for me." *(STRONGLAND buyer.)* → PB-004's day-21 checkpoint, floor habits and Restart Page exist because of this line.
11. **Printable trackers get loved and pinned up.** "The worksheet provided with purchase is a simple, yet effective touch." · "Hang it up on my office and fill it the next day." *(STRONGLAND buyers.)* → every program ships a pin-up artefact; PB-004's wall tracker is a headline deliverable, not a bonus.
12. **The values and vision layer is what buyers praise.** "…effort into the general vision, core values, wheel of life and your general goals…" *(Daniel Marthi buyer.)* → the compass and values work leads PB-002 rather than sitting at the back.

## Design and listing craft

13. **Thumbnails are interchangeable.** 12 of 15 flagship thumbnails carry a Notion logo or badge, 11 show a device, most use grey/black/blue sans. **Exactly one warm, light, editorial thumbnail exists in the whole set.** *(Report §Thumbnail search-grid test; `research/audits/thumbs-gumroad-top.png`.)*

![The fifteen flagship thumbnails at search-grid size — the sameness is the opportunity](../research/audits/thumbs-gumroad-top.png)

14. **Image sets are thin even on expensive products.** Easlo's $79 Second Brain has 2 images; Chris's $149 Life OS has 1 still image; The M.E.A.N. Workbook at $47 has a cover and nothing else. *(Report §Executive summary #9; `research/audits/gumroad-theoverwhelmedbrain-jkSo.md`.)* → the 10-slot set is a cheap, durable edge.
15. **Consistency wins the shop grid.** The strongest shops use one cover template across every product; Easlo has 24,310 ratings on a single monochrome formula. *(Report §Seller profiles 05.)* → one cover system, one accent colour per product family.

![The same fifteen products as 16:9 hero covers](../research/audits/heroes-gumroad-top.png)

## The closest competitor, studied properly

Ideas To Thrive is the only seller in our target quadrant. Its paid workbooks are genuinely well made — editorial serif titles, letter-spaced kickers, spec strips, one framework per page, worked examples, designed for home printing at low ink.

![Ideas To Thrive — Build Your Discipline System, the full listing sequence](../research/assets/gumroad/ideastothrive/discipline-system/_sheet.png)

**What we borrow as principles** (never wording or layout): design for bad days (floor + restart); diagnose before you plan; one framework per page with a fixed anatomy; multi-format by default; spec strips and page-anatomy images. *(`research/sellers/gumroad-S3-ideastothrive.md` §G.)*

**Where we beat it:** it has no ladder, no bundles, almost no proof, inconsistent naming, a cool-grey free-kit system that clashes with its warm paid system, and free kits that "look like web forms". We ship a coherent ladder, a warmer and more distinctive identity, our own diagrams, and designed free products.

## What the data can't tell us

- **No review text.** Gumroad exposes star breakdowns only; every buyer quote above is a testimonial the seller chose to publish. Complaint reasons behind 1★ ratings are unknown.
- **No free-to-paid split** is published anywhere, so conversion is always an assumption (the report models 2% / 5% / 10%).
- **Hidden sales** on many paid products (Easlo, Organized dashboard, Sireal, Daniel Marthi, most Ideas To Thrive paid workbooks), so several competitors are understated.
- **Etsy is entirely absent** from this evidence base. Tablet-planner demand, which is probably Etsy-first, is therefore unproven for us.
- **Nothing here measures our own audience,** because we don't have one yet. That is the single biggest risk in this plan.

# Brand foundation

Full kit: **`Product Plans/brand-kit.md` (v1)**. Summary below.

> [!WARNING] **The brand name is an [Assumption] pending the owner's confirmation.** Everything else — palette, type, page system, voice — stands regardless of which name is chosen. Only the wordmark changes.

## The choice: Northing Studio

The report offered three directions. We **merge them into one brand** rather than picking one, because the evidence says shop-grid consistency is what compounds:

- **"Northing Studio"** gives the **name, palette and compass device** — a calm, reflective identity that reads instantly as *not another Notion dashboard*.
- **"Field Manual"** gives the **page craft**: letter-spaced kickers, spec strips, worked examples, one framework per page. This is the part that makes a $19 PDF feel worth $19.
- **"1% Studio"** gives the **method**: floors, restarts, the Kaizen loop, which becomes PB-004 and Part 3 of PB-002.

**Justification from the positioning map.** The map's open cell is *warm, light, editorial × guided method*. "Northing Studio" is the only one of the three names that signals **both** halves — warmth and direction — while "1% Studio" signals method but reads cool and techy, and "Field Manual" signals method but is generic and already echoed by the incumbent's "A working manual" subtitle.

**Conflict screens (search only — NOT legal clearance; a professional trademark search is required before the owner commits):**

- **Northing Studio** — no Etsy shop, Gumroad creator or product found under the exact name; no USPTO record surfaced. **Proceed.**
- **1% Studio** — **rejected**: `1percnt.com`, `onepercentstudio.com`, and **The 1% Planner**, an existing undated goal-planner brand. Three live users, one in our category.
- **Field Manual** — **rejected as a brand**: generic, unownable, and too close to the incumbent's language.

## Palette

`#FBF8F3` paper · `#1F2A36` ink · `#3E6B89` primary · `#D9A441` accent · `#7C9A7E` support · `#E4DCD0` stone

| Role | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Paper | `#FBF8F3` | every page and listing-image ground |
| Ink | `#1F2A36` | all body text, prompts, rules, writing lines |
| Primary | `#3E6B89` | headings, tabs, kickers, diagram strokes, the compass |
| Accent | `#D9A441` | one focal element per image; badges; the "you are here" marker. **Never text.** ≤10% of any image |
| Support | `#7C9A7E` | Kaizen and growth marks, floor chips, charts |
| Stone | `#E4DCD0` | worked-example boxes, zebra rows, quiet panels |
| Print variant | — | white ground, hairline rules, tints not solids, **≤8% ink coverage** |

Chosen against the measured competitor palettes: greyscale minimal, corporate steel blue, dark neon, terracotta boho, burgundy, navy+yellow and editorial jewel are all taken. **Warm paper + deep ink + one confident accent is almost unused, and it prints well** — which matters because every product is a printable.

**Accessibility rules:** body text is always ink on paper. Primary is for headings ≥18 pt only. Accent never carries meaning alone — always paired with a label or shape, so pages survive greyscale and colour-blindness. Greyscale legibility is a QA gate on every product.

## Voice

**Calm · specific · kind-but-firm.** Deliberately the opposite of the two loudest sellers in the category: STRONGLAND's shaming, male-coded copy with weight-loss claims, and The Overwhelmed Brain's diagnostic-sounding promises with no disclaimer.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Twenty minutes a day, for seven days." | "Transform your entire life in one week!" |
| "A bad week is data, not failure. Here's the restart page." | "No excuses. Most people quit here." |
| "This is paper and PDF. You do the thinking." | "This system does the work for you." |
| Name our own mechanisms: the Floor, the Restart Page, the Vital Few | Borrowed method names (GTD, PARA, WOOP, Wheel of Life) |

## Visual system and listing rules

- **Signature device:** our own **four-direction compass** — N Direction · E Energy · S Systems · W Wellbeing — used as icon, diagram and scoring wheel. **Never the ikigai Venn**, which is borrowed and widely copied.
- **Second device:** the **Kaizen loop** — Floor → Ideal → Restart.
- **The thumbnail formula we will own:** warm paper ground → mono kicker → **one Fraunces benefit line of ≤6 words** → one line-art device with a single brass marker → a thin spec strip. No device mockup, no third-party logos, no busy desk scene.
- **One accent per family:** Focus = brass · Direction = primary · Kaizen = sage · Reflection = stone.
- **Every product ships all 10 image slots.** No proof numbers until we actually have them; no fake urgency; no platform-endorsement badges.

# Product line architecture

## The ladder

The report's observed Gumroad ladder runs free → $5–9 → $17–29 → $39–59 → $79–99 → bundles. We deliberately stop at $49: our categories are thin-but-real, we have no audience yet, and the evidence shows **cheap bundles outsell expensive ones** (Daniel Marthi's $29 bundle: 204 sales, against Heyismail's $325 bundle: 111).

| Rung | Price | Products | Job on the ladder |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead magnet | **free** | PB-006 One-Page Year · PB-022 10-Minute Life Audit | Ratings, email, brand recognition. Each is a complete tool, not a crippled demo |
| Entry | **$9–12** | PB-001 Focus Audit · PB-003 Balance Audit | Impulse price, proven band ($7–$17 guided PDFs), fast to build |
| Core system | **$19–29** | PB-002 The Compass System (+Notion tier) | The flagship and the brand's argument |
| Program | **$24** | PB-004 90-Day Kaizen Program | Proven format ($19.99 × 13,089), obvious design gap |
| Retention | **$15** | PB-005 The Review Ritual | Brings buyers back weekly; the reason to stay subscribed |
| Bundle | **$49** | BN-02 The Life Direction Set | Cross-sell from every listing |

**Each free product upgrades into a specific paid one** — PB-006 → PB-002, PB-022 → PB-003 — following the pattern that works across the top sellers (free tools as modules of the paid system), but with an honest "this free version is complete as it is" line, borrowed as a principle from Organized dashboard's "what this free version doesn't do".

## Platform strategy

**Gumroad now.** Per listing: a 16:9 benefit hero, a separate square thumbnail, the 10-slot image set, an FAQ that answers the price objection, a stated guarantee, a changelog line, and a polite rating request in the delivery file. Ratings drive Discover ranking, so **the free products exist partly to farm ratings honestly**.

**Free-tier structure is an open question for the owner** (see Open questions Q3). The evidence supports both: free tiers inside paid listings drive ranking and email capture (the top 7 volume sellers all use them), but they also make published sales numbers meaningless and can cannibalise a $9 product. **Recommendation [Assumption]: separate free listings for PB-006 and PB-022, paid listings kept clean** — because our paid products are cheap already, and because a $0 tier caps the whole listing at Gumroad's 250 MB free-product limit.

**Etsy later.** Every brief below carries an Etsy-ready title (≤140 characters) and 13 tags (≤20 characters each) so nothing needs rewriting when Etsy research resumes. File plan respects Etsy's limit of **5 files × 20 MB**, which is why each product ships as a small number of consolidated PDFs rather than many.

## Naming system

`The <Noun>` for tools and systems — The Focus Audit, The Balance Audit, The Compass System, The Review Ritual. Programs carry their duration: The 90-Day Kaizen Program. Free products state their size or time: The One-Page Year, The 10-Minute Life Audit.

The word **Compass** is the house word: it appears in the brand, the flagship and the signature device, tying the catalogue together the way "[Limitless OS]" ties Daniel Marthi's freebies to his flagship — without putting a bracketed system tag in every title.

## Pricing rationale

| Decision | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Entry at **$9–12** | Guided PDF products sit at $7–$47; *7-Day Reset* $7 × 2,272 and *My Ikigai* $17 × 785 bracket our range |
| Flagship at **$19**, Notion tier **$29** | *Live Intentionally* $19.99 × 13,089 is the best-selling guided PDF in the study; Maria Whitsett's $27 Notion planner did 16,735. $19/$29 sits inside both |
| Program at **$24** | Priced above the $19.99 comparable because ours includes a designed wall tracker and 70 designed pages, and below $29 to stay impulse-adjacent |
| Bundle at **$49** | Cheap bundles outsell expensive ones: Daniel Marthi $29 → 204 sales; Heyismail $325 → 111, $159 → 82; Rosidssoy $149 → 21 |
| **Launch discounts** of ~20% for two weeks, then they end | Anchoring is common but usually dishonest — permanent "50% off Black Friday" labels and stale sale banners appear across several shops. A discount that actually expires is a differentiator |
| **A stated guarantee** | Guarantees are rare — only Daniel Marthi, STRONGLAND and one Atul product offer one, and most shops say "No refunds". **Recommendation [Assumption]: a plain 30-day, no-questions refund**, which also fits Gumroad's own policy expectations |
| **No $79–$149 tier** | Those prices exist only where the seller has an audience (Easlo, Chris, Daniel Marthi). We have none yet |

## Revenue scenarios — planning arithmetic, not forecasts

## The lead-magnet funnel

Free product → Gumroad email capture → a five-email welcome sequence → the matching paid product. The two free products point at different paid products, so the funnel branches rather than competing with itself.

**Five-email welcome sequence (outline):**

1. **Delivery + one instruction.** The file, plus a single line on how to actually use it today. No pitch. A polite rating request at the bottom.
2. **Day 2 — the method behind the page.** Explain the floor idea, or the honest-scoring rules. Pure value; teach the mechanism we named.
3. **Day 4 — a worked example.** One real, specific example of the tool in use, including where it went wrong. This is the trust email.
4. **Day 7 — the natural next step.** Introduce the matching paid product with its honest limits ("this is paper and PDF"), the launch price if one is live, and the guarantee.
5. **Day 14 — the ritual.** How to make the habit stick without buying anything, plus one cross-link and a genuine question asking what they're struggling with (this is our only source of buyer voice, since Gumroad gives us none).

## Content angles

The research is unambiguous that Gumroad winners are audience-led, and our revenue scenarios turn almost entirely on this. Angles that suit the brand and reuse work already done:

- **"Show the page"** — a single page of a product, explained: what it asks and why. Endlessly repeatable, and it doubles as listing-image work.
- **"The floor"** — short posts on the smallest version of a habit that still counts. This is our most distinctive idea and the one buyers respond to.
- **"What the tracker doesn't tell you"** — gently contrarian pieces on why trackers without a review ritual fail. Positions us against the whole category without naming anyone.
- **"A year of shapes"** — the twelve mini-wheels concept, shown over time. Visual, and it sells PB-003 on sight.
- **"Restart"** — content published deliberately in mid-February and late September, when people have already fallen off.

## Intellectual property — the hard rules

1. **WHEEL OF LIFE® is a live US registration** (Reg. 3918518, Ser. 77779648), originally Meyer Resource Group Inc., now **Success Motivation International, Inc.**, covering *books, booklets, manuals and printed instructional materials relating to personal development, self-motivation and improvement, goal setting, planning, leadership and management* — **our exact goods**. 2. **"Life Compass" is crowded and clinically associated** — multiple Etsy listings, at least one Gumroad product, a "Life Compass Planner" line, and it is the name of an **ACT (therapy) values-clarification worksheet**. PB-002 was renamed from "Life Compass OS" to **The Compass System**.

## Claims

- **Money:** every money-touching page and listing carries *"Educational only — not financial advice."* PB-003's Money area and PB-005's spending question both carry the footnote.
- **Audits stay non-clinical.** We score satisfaction and attention, never symptoms. No "burnout", "anxiety", "depression", "diagnosis", "assessment", "healing" or "symptoms" language. Every audit carries: *"A structured self-reflection tool, not a diagnosis, assessment or therapy"*, plus an encouragement to talk to a professional.
- **PB-004 makes behavioural claims only** — no weight, calorie, sleep-disorder or medication content, including in sample habits. This is precisely where the category's best-seller takes on risk.
- **No fabricated proof.** No sales counts, testimonial walls or "join 200+ others" lines until they are true, and no platform-endorsement badges.

## Accessibility

- Body text is ink on warm paper at ≥9 pt, never below.
- Colour never carries meaning alone — every accent is paired with a label or shape.
- **Every product must be legible in pure greyscale**, which is a QA gate on all seven specs (it also serves the ink-light promise).
- Writing lines at 7.5 mm are tested with a real pen at print size, not judged on screen.
- Tablet files are tested in GoodNotes and Notability; Samsung Notes is only claimed if it is actually tested.

# Open questions for the owner

Ranked by how much they block the build.

1. **Is the brand "Northing Studio"?** Everything else in this plan survives a different name — palette, type, page system and voice are independent of it — but the wordmark, file names and listing copy all wait on this. Our screen found no conflicting shop, product or USPTO record, but **a professional trademark search is still required**, and it should cover "Kaizen" for PB-004 at the same time. *Blocks: all listing copy, week 1.*
2. **Notion tiers at launch, or later?** They add **22 hours** and about $10 per product in price, and they reach Gumroad's dominant buyer group — but every hour there is an hour not spent on the printable line that actually differentiates us. **Our recommendation [Assumption]: ship print + tablet first, add Notion to PB-003 and PB-002 once each has its first ten paid sales.** *Blocks: PB-003 spec scope, week 5.*
3. **Free tiers inside paid listings, or separate free products?** The evidence genuinely supports both, which is why it is Experiment 1. Our recommendation is separate free listings, because a $0 tier caps the whole listing at 250 MB and our paid prices are already low. *Blocks: listing structure, week 2.*
4. **Which audience channel will you actually sustain?** This is the question that decides the revenue scenarios — the gap between the low and high cases is almost entirely audience, not product. Newsletter, YouTube, Pinterest and Threads all work in the evidence, but only for people who kept going. **Pick one you can do weekly for a year.** *Blocks: nothing in the build; decides the outcome.*
5. **Do we offer a 30-day guarantee?** Rare in the category and therefore a real differentiator, but it carries refund risk on a $9 product where the buyer keeps the file. Our recommendation is yes, plainly stated. *Blocks: listing copy, week 4.*

**Also worth a decision, but not blocking:** when does Etsy research resume (tablet planners are probably Etsy-first, and PB-024/PB-025 wait on it)? Will the owner's name and face appear on the brand (founder-led shops convert well in the evidence, but it is a personal choice)? And should the launch discounts run at all, given that honest pricing is part of our positioning?
