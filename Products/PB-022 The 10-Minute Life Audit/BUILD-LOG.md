## 2. The eight-area wheel (the asset PB-003 and PB-002 will reuse)

Built as a vector master with editable text labels: `build/assets/wheel.svg`,
generated by `build/wheel.py` so the geometry is computed, not eyeballed.

- 1 viewBox unit = 1 mm at 164 mm rendered width, so the outer ring is
  **exactly 120 mm across** on A4, as the spec requires.
- 8 spokes, primary, 1.25 pt. 5 hairline stone rings at scores 2/4/6/8/10.
- Tick marks at 1/3/5/7/9 on every spoke, so all eleven integers are findable
  with a pen — the rings alone only locate the even scores.
- Ring numbers sit in the empty wedge between the N and NE spokes, on
  paper-coloured backing rects that cut the ring line cleanly. Paper costs no
  ink, so this is free.
- Labels are real `<text>`, editable, and the whole device is our own drawing.

**It is not the four-circle ikigai Venn.** The trademarked phrase for this kind
of diagram appears in no product file, file name, label, header or alt text —
verified by `grep -ril` across the whole product folder, which returns nothing.

## 3. Letter and tablet are genuine re-layouts, not scaled A4

Letter is 5.9 mm wider and 17.6 mm shorter than A4. The scoring row is
re-flowed rather than squashed: on A4 the "why" rule spans the full row width
beneath the scale strip; on Letter and tablet it tucks under the area name
*beside* the scale strip, using the extra width to buy back the lost height.

The tablet sheet (1620 × 2160 px = 428.6 × 571.5 mm) is scaled by a single
`zoom: 2.041` on an inner `.sheet` wrapper, so one tablet page is the optical
equal of an A4 page — type, rules and the wheel all keep their paper
proportions instead of me re-specifying twenty type sizes by hand.

**This FAIL is a gate artefact, and no product copy is missing.** The cause is
deterministic and worth recording, because it will recur in every spec:

`required_strings()` pulls quoted runs with `"([^"]{12,})"`. The page-3 cell
opens with `Title: "Your wheel"`, and `Your wheel` is **10 characters — below
the 12-character floor**. That pair is therefore skipped, and every subsequent
quote in the cell pairs one position out of step: the closing quote of
`"Your wheel"` becomes the *opening* quote of the next match, capturing the
spec's own connecting prose `. Instruction:` instead of the instruction text,
and then ` Below the wheel, three short fields: ` instead of `"Lowest area:"`.

So the gate is demanding two fragments of **editorial prose from the spec
document**, which would be nonsense printed on a page — while the actual
instruction text and `"Lowest area:"` were never checked at all.

I verified directly that every genuine page-3 string is in the PDF:

```
  PRESENT 'STEP TWO · SEE IT'
  PRESENT 'Your wheel'
  PRESENT 'Mark each score on its spoke, then join the marks. …the dents.'
  PRESENT 'Lowest area:'
  PRESENT 'Highest area:'
  PRESENT 'The one that surprised me:'
  PRESENT "A dented wheel is normal. A dent you've named is workable."
```

I did **not** print `. Instruction:` on the page to turn the gate green. Bending
the product's copy to satisfy a regex would defeat the purpose of the gate.
Owner decision required — see §7, Q4.

## 6. A defect in the *shared* stylesheet — needs an owner decision

**CSS `opacity` on a text element silently corrupts the PDF text layer.**

`product-base.css` sets `.panel .kicker { opacity: .75 }`. The panel kicker
"IF YOU WANT MORE" extracted from the PDF as `I F  Y O U  W A N T  M O R E` —
unsearchable, un-copyable by the buyer, and invisible to the copy gate, which
reported it MISSING.

I isolated the cause rather than guessing. Five identical mono lines, one
variable changed at a time:

```
'AAA CONTROL TRACKING ONLY'                        ← tracking only          OK
'EEE TRACKING PLUS COLOUR'                         ← tracking + colour      OK
'B B B  T R A C K I N G  P L U S  O P A C I T Y'   ← tracking + opacity   BROKEN
'C C C  L E S S  T R A C K I N G  P L U S  …'      ← less tracking+opacity BROKEN
'D D D  O P A C I T Y  O N L Y  N O  T R A C …'    ← opacity alone        BROKEN
```

Any `opacity` breaks it; tracking never does. Chrome emits the run inside a
transparency group with per-glyph positioning.

**Scope:** this is not a PB-022 bug. Three rules in the shared
`Products/_assets/product-base.css` are affected and will silently damage the
text layer of *any* product that uses them:

- `.panel .kicker { opacity: .75 }`
- `.disclaimer { opacity: .8 }`
- `.matrix .axis { opacity: .7 }`

I fixed it **locally** in `build/product.css` (full opacity, quiet tone carried
by colour instead) and did **not** edit the shared asset, because that would
change every future product without the owner asking. PB-022 avoids
`.disclaimer` and `.matrix` entirely. Recommended fix for the shared file:
replace each `opacity` with an explicit colour. See §7, Q5.

## 7. `[Assumption]`s and open questions

### Assumptions made in the build

1. **`[Assumption]` The brand name "Quiet Compass" is not confirmed.** Still the
   owner's open decision. It is held in one constant (`BRAND` in
   `make_pages.py`) and appears only as copy — never in a class name, a file
   name, or the wheel artwork. A rename is a one-line change plus a re-export.
   *The PDF file names do contain "QuietCompass" — renaming means re-exporting
   the three files, which is cheap, but the listing links would change.*
2. **The footer disclaimer is set at 9 pt, not the 8 pt the spec asks for.** The
   9 pt floor is a hard accessibility gate in both `printable-pdf` and the
   builder's own rules. The spec is impossible as written here, so I built the
   nearest honest thing and am recording it rather than silently shrinking type
   below the floor.
3. **Wheel labels are 9.5 pt, not the spec's 9 pt** — 9 pt sits exactly on the
   gate floor and risks failing on rounding. 0.5 pt of margin, no visual cost.
4. **The Letter wheel is 114 mm across, not 120 mm** (152 mm on tablet). The
   Letter sheet is 17.6 mm shorter than A4; at a full 120 mm the closing line
   clipped the trim. A4 — the primary print edition — keeps the specified
   120 mm exactly.
5. **All five page-4 fields have 2 ruled lines.** Within the spec's "1–2 lines
   each", chosen to fill the column honestly (see §5.2).
6. **The zebra is stone at 55%, not solid stone.** `brand-kit.md` names "table
   zebra rows" as a use of stone but also mandates an ink-light printable; 55%
   keeps the banding legible at 2.5% average ink.
7. **Margins are 15 mm all round** per the spec's "Page setup", overriding the
   shared base's 18 mm head.
8. **No tab rail on the tablet edition.** The generic builder instructions say
   the tablet build adds one; this spec explicitly says "No tab rail — at four
   pages it would be decoration." The spec wins on content.

### 11.2 [MAJOR] p.3 — the wheel was numbered on one spoke of eight — **FIXED**

**What changed.** The scale is repeated in **all eight wedges**: 40 numbers
(8 × `2 4 6 8 10`) instead of 5. Every spoke now has a complete 0–10 reference

and PB-002 will reuse.** Round 1 cut the ring line with a paper-coloured backing

ring in eight places across a 75 mm circumference — it would have read as a
dashed circle, and the rings are already the faintest thing on the page. So the
numbers now sit **2.2 mm inside the ring they label** and the backing rects are
gone entirely. Each number is 2.2 mm from its own ring and 3.8 mm from the
odd-score tick below it, so which one it belongs to is unambiguous, and **all
five rings are now unbroken circles.**

**Evidence — geometry.** `build/assets/wheel.svg` carries 48 `<text>` elements =
40 scale numbers + 8 area labels; `<rect>` count is **0**; `<circle>` count is 6
(5 rings + the hub dot).

**Evidence — looked at.** `qa/pages/…A4-p03.png`, `…Letter-p03.png`,
`…Tablet-p03.png` and the A4 greyscale twin, all opened this session. All eight
labels are complete and unclipped (HEALTH WORK MONEY PEOPLE HOME GROWTH PLAY
DIRECTION). The numbers read as a regular instrument face rather than clutter.

**Is it genuinely markable on all eight spokes? Yes — with one honest limit.**
To put PEOPLE at 7 you now read `6` and `8` in the wedge immediately beside the
PEOPLE spoke, roughly 10 mm away, and the tick between them is the 7. No score
requires carrying a position across the circle. The limit: the numbers sit in
the wedges, not *on* the spoke, so the last 22.5° is still eye-work — that is
deliberate, because numbers printed on the spoke would sit exactly where the
buyer's mark and joining line go. **Whether a real pen confirms this is a human
check** (§13).

**Unexpected greyscale benefit.** In `…A4-p03-grey.png` the rings still read
faint (the round-1 MINOR), but the 40 numbers read clearly in pure greyscale.
A buyer whose laser printer loses the hairline rings can still find every score,
